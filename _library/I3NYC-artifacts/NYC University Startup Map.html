How New York City universities back startups and researchers

A map of the incubators, accelerators, venture-building programs, proof-of-concept funds and seed funds run by New York City's universities and academic medical centers: who they serve, at what stage, with how much money, on what terms, whether they are running today, and what their alumni went on to do.

Designed by Andrea Calcagno, Vice Chair, Board Member & Co-Founder, I³/NYC

What the map shows

Seven findings from the programs below. Every figure comes from the program's own page or a university release, linked in the database.

  • The serious money sits in life sciences proof-of-concept

    Columbia's Lab-to-Market network has made 437 cash awards worth $62.5M, and its Deerfield partnership commits up to $130M over ten years. Rockefeller started its therapeutic fund with $25M and Mount Sinai seeded its i3 Accelerator with $10M. Nothing comparable exists for software.

  • Most doors require a university badge

    Programs fund their own students, faculty and alumni. A minority accept founders with no affiliation: Endless Frontier Labs, Runway, Tandon Future Labs, Elementa Labs, MSK's iHub and the lab-space incubators.

  • Non-dilutive is the default

    Grants and prizes dominate. Published equity terms appear in four places: Cornell Tech's Runway (a $175K SAFE at a $3M cap), Columbia's Lang Fund (a convertible note), the NYU Innovation Venture Fund and the NYU Langone Health Venture Fund.

  • Researchers follow one standard path: NSF I-Corps

    CUNY leads the regional hub with Columbia and NYU, on a $15M award that ends December 31, 2026. NYU packages the path into a ladder worth up to $102K per team.

  • The track record is real, and concentrated

    Endless Frontier Labs graduates have raised $3.20B. Cornell Tech startups have raised over $500M and are valued above $1.3B. Tandon Future Labs counts 40 acquisitions worth $940M+.

  • Programs get renamed more often than they close

    Weill Cornell's Daedalus Fund became the Enterprise Innovation Catalyst Fund in spring 2025. Rockefeller's Robertson fund is now carried by the Black family gift of 2024. Mount Sinai's i3 Genesis track is unavailable.

  • Two public levers are shut for now

    START-UP NY closed to new applicants on January 1, 2026. IN2NYC, the CUNY visa route for international entrepreneurs, lists applications as closed with no reopening date.

Entry points for international founders and researchers

The programs that do not require a New York university affiliation, by what they are good for.

Science and deep-tech startups, already incorporated

Endless Frontier Labs (NYU Stern) admits startups from anywhere, takes no equity and charges no fees. The CEO attends five meeting days over nine months, two of them in person in New York. Applications run mid-May to end of July.

Recent PhDs who want to found in New York

Runway (Cornell Tech) recruits PhD founders from universities worldwide and gives salary, research budget, housing allowance and space for 12 to 24 months, against a SAFE.

Digital health startups seeking a US hospital partner

Elementa Labs (Mount Sinai) runs 12-week cohorts for pre-seed and seed companies; over a third of applicants are international. MSK's iHub Challenge runs three-month cohorts with Memorial Sloan Kettering teams. Neither provides cash.

AI, data, climate and urban-tech startups

NYU Tandon Future Labs in Downtown Brooklyn runs sector incubators and hosts programs with foreign partners, including Innovate UK's global incubator programme.

Biotech teams that need a bench

BioLabs@NYULangone in Manhattan, the Einstein incubator in the Bronx and SUNY Downstate's incubator and BioBAT in Brooklyn rent wet-lab space to early-stage companies.

A visa route that is closed for now

IN2NYC pairs an international entrepreneur with a partner school so the founder can qualify for a cap-exempt H-1B visa. NYCEDC lists applications as closed and says future rounds will be advertised.

Method and caveats

  • Scope: programs run by, or housed at, universities and academic medical centers in the five boroughs. City-run programs and private accelerators are excluded.
  • Status was checked on October 10, 2026 against each program's own page or a dated university release. The evidence is quoted in each row.
  • "Listed" means the official page is live but shows no 2025–26 date. "Unconfirmed" means I found only older sources: BioLabs@NYULangone (2019), Tri-I TDI (2020), Pace (2018) and the Weill business plan challenge (2019).
  • Three official sites could not be opened during the check: the NY I-Corps Hub site, the IN2NYC site and the BioLabs site. Their rows rely on NSF, NYCEDC and NYU Langone pages instead.
  • Access labels are my reading of published eligibility. "Mixed" means affiliates lead but outsiders can join a team, or the page states no rule.
  • Success cases are self-reported by programs. Attribution to a program does not mean the program caused the outcome.
  • Check sizes are not published for the NYU Innovation Venture Fund, the NYU Langone funds, the Weill Catalyst Fund, MSK's fund and i3 Accelerator projects.
  • BiomedX's average award (about $74K) is my division of $6M by 81 awards, not a published figure.
  • Not covered for lack of a verifiable source: Yeshiva, Pratt, FIT and St. John's programs.
