New York City's private accelerators, incubators and venture studios

A map of the privately run programs that fund, house and build startups in New York City: what each one invests and takes, who can get in, whether it is running today, and what its alumni went on to do. It is the companion to the map of university programs.

Designed by Andrea Calcagno, Vice Chair, Board Member & Co-Founder, I³/NYC

What the map shows

Seven findings from the programs below. Every figure comes from the operator's own page or a dated release, linked in the database.

  • Private money is equity money

    The standard deals are published: Techstars NYC puts in $220K (5% plus an uncapped SAFE), ERA $150K for 6%, South Park Commons $400K for 7% with $600K more guaranteed, and Antler commits $500K–$1M. University programs, by contrast, mostly give grants.

  • A few strong programs take nothing

    The Grand Central Tech Residency gives a year of space for zero rent and zero equity. The FinTech Innovation Lab and the NYC AI Landing Pad are free and non-dilutive. Betaworks Camp charges no fees.

  • Open to the world, but the visa is yours

    Techstars, Betaworks and South Park Commons take founders from anywhere. Antler requires US work authorization and the FinTech Innovation Lab states it will not sponsor visas. No private program in this map solves immigration.

  • The city co-produces much of the supply

    NYCEDC funds the NYC AI Nexus (C10 Labs, Plug and Play) and the International Landing Pad Network (Plug and Play, Supermomos, SOSA, The Clean Fight), and partners with Newlab, Company Ventures and Visible Hands on founder fellowships.

  • Studios produce the largest outcomes

    AlleyCorp incubated MongoDB, Business Insider and Gilt Groupe. Redesign Health and its portfolio have raised over $1.5B since 2018. Studios recruit founders; they do not run open cohorts.

  • International founders have a dedicated lane

    Five programs exist to land foreign companies: the AI Landing Pad (for companies with $5M in revenue or funding), The Clean Fight's landing pad, SOSA, VentureOut, and the Italian Trade Agency's Global Start Up Program run with gener8tor.

  • Lists go stale fast

    URBAN-X closed at the end of 2025 after 15 cohorts. IndieBio NY became SOSV NY in 2026. Entrepreneur First lists no New York program. Lair East Labs' site has not changed since 2020.

Entry points for international and Italian founders

Which door fits, by the company's situation.

Italian innovative startup, first US exposure

The ITA Global Start Up Program placed ten Italian startups with gener8tor in New York in autumn 2025: six weeks, two virtual and four in person. The 2026 edition runs September to November and is free for registered innovative startups.

AI company with $5M in revenue or funding

The NYC AI Landing Pad (Supermomos with Lightspeed) gives twelve months of coworking in Manhattan, mentorship and investor meetings, with no cost and no equity.

Early-stage team that can relocate for three months

Techstars NYC and Betaworks Camp accept founders based anywhere and require in-person weeks in New York. South Park Commons runs its fellowship from a New York office.

Climate or energy company ready to deploy

The Clean Fight runs an International Landing Pad cohort of ten growth-stage companies and administers deployment grants in New York State.

Fintech with a product banks can test

The FinTech Innovation Lab puts about ten companies in front of 40+ financial institutions for twelve weeks. Core team must work in New York and hold US work rights.

A short, structured market visit

VentureOut runs one-week sector programs and three-month US launch programs for foreign startups, often with a home-country government as sponsor.

Method and caveats

  • Scope: privately operated accelerators, incubators, venture studios and landing programs with a New York City program or site. Corporate and nonprofit operators are included; university and purely city-run programs are in the companion map.
  • Status was checked on October 10, 2026 against each operator's own site or a dated release. The evidence is quoted in each row.
  • Five official sites could not be read during the check: Company Ventures, URBAN-X, JLABS, Plug and Play's New York page and Morgan Stanley's venture lab. Their rows rely on the secondary sources linked, or the program is left out.
  • Left out because a New York base or program could not be confirmed from the operator's own pages: Expa, Juxtapose, Forum Ventures, Interplay, Prehype, Kairos, HearstLab and the Morgan Stanley lab.
  • Access labels are my reading of published eligibility. "Conditions apply" covers work-authorization rules, founder-profile criteria and size thresholds.
  • Several operators publish only global figures (Techstars, Antler, Founder Institute, IndieBio). These are marked and are not New York results.
  • XRC's $135K figure dates from its March 2022 cohort; Lair East Labs' $50K from a site last updated in 2020.
  • Success cases are self-reported. Attribution to a program does not mean the program caused the outcome.
