Italy's AI and physical AI companies

A census of 74 AI and physical AI companies headquartered in Italy that have closed at least a seed round. Each profile records what the company sells and to whom, whose models it runs on, its funding, scale, its base in Italy and one main use case with impact indicators. A companion to the I³/NYC census of New York City.

What the census shows

Eight findings computed from the profiles below. The census is a curated sample of Italy's funded AI companies, not a complete register; see the method notes for what is missing.

  • Two companies hold most of the capital

    €750M of the €1.39B disclosed went to Exein (€420M) and Domyn (€330M of equity), about 54%. The median company has raised €5.6M.

  • A seed-stage market

    39 of 74 companies are at seed and 20 at Series A. Only six private companies have reached Series B or later, and two (Exein, Domyn) are valued at €1B or more.

  • Milan is the hub, Turin the second pole

    36 companies are based in Milan, with €654M raised. Turin has 8, most of them out of the Politecnico's I3P incubator. Rome's €432M is almost all Exein.

  • Physical AI is Italy's distinctive bet

    11 physical AI companies have raised €188M. Generative Bionics' €70M seed for humanoids was described as the largest academic spin-off round in Europe; Niulinx raised €38M for autonomous driving.

  • The state is the anchor investor

    CDP Venture Capital sits on 20 of 74 cap tables and is among the lead investors in 14. Private and foreign leads appear mainly in the larger rounds: Headline in Exein, Left Lane in Lexroom, United Ventures in Smartness.

  • 2026 is the biggest year so far

    28 companies raised their latest round in 2026: about €481M of equity, plus Domyn's package of about €950M, mostly debt. In 2025 it was 21 companies and €197M. Nationally, 77% of software rounds in 2026 were AI-linked.

  • Italian companies build their own models

    Of the 42 companies that disclose a model strategy, 28 train in-house, often vision, forecasting or robotics models rather than LLMs. Only 7 build mainly on external models. Domyn and Expert.ai develop their own language technology.

  • Exits are small and mostly strategic

    Qualcomm bought Arduino, TeamSystem bought Normo.ai, Stirling Square bought iConsulting for over €200M. Italy's largest listing of the period, Bending Spoons, went to Nasdaq.

    Method and limits

    • Scope. Companies whose product is AI or depends on it, with headquarters in Italy, and at least one disclosed seed or later round (about €500K or more) or €2M or more raised in total. Older platforms that added AI are included and labelled AI-enabled.
    • Headquarters test. A company counts when its legal and operating headquarters are in Italy. Italian-founded companies headquartered in London, the US, Switzerland or the Netherlands are listed separately, not counted.
    • Not exhaustive. Italy counts nearly 15,000 AI-focused firms. This census profiles 74 of the funded ones that could be verified within a fixed research budget. Names that were reviewed and left out are listed below with the reason.
    • Currency. Amounts are in euros. Rounds announced in dollars are converted at about $1 = €0.86, so figures can differ slightly from press headlines.
    • Sources. Company announcements, CDP Venture Capital, Growth Capital and Italian Tech Alliance, Dealroom, tech.eu, EU-Startups, StartupItalia, Startupbusiness, Milano Finanza, Teleborsa and trade press. Each profile links its sources.
    • Confidence. 18 profiles are rated high and 17 low. Low means the location, the founding year or the funding rests on a single aggregator. Low-confidence rows carry a label.
    • Funding gaps. 10 companies have no total-raised figure, so sums understate. Domyn's total is equity only; its reported 2026 package is mostly debt. Valuations are rarely disclosed in Italy: only Exein and Domyn have a reported one.
    • Revenue and staff are thin. Only 5 companies publish a revenue figure and 16 a headcount; these are mostly company statements or LinkedIn counts.
    • Model strategy. Coded from what companies and press say. 32 companies disclose nothing usable and are left as undisclosed.
    • Websites. Company site links are shown only where the domain was confirmed; others are left out rather than guessed.
    • Dates. Research closed October 16, 2026. Founding year is missing for 22 companies.
